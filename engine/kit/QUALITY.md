# The bar

A technically correct edit can still look cheap. Small, static, timid graphics are what make a video read as amateur, so hold every edit to this before anyone sees it.

- **Scale.** Type is as wide as the frame, not a label. If something could be 40% bigger, make it bigger. A phone is small.
- **Depth.** The big things go behind the head, where the head breaks them. That overlap reads as produced. Things on top are things you'd touch: a prompt bar, a comment, a card.
- **A camera.** Open on a small move, punch in on the lines that matter, push slowly while type stands still behind them. A locked camera reads as a screen recording.
- **One designed moment per beat.** A cover with a masthead and a barcode beats a chip and a strip. One idea, readable in a second or two.
- **The edit does what the words say.** "It warms up" warms the picture. "Huge" goes huge. An effect with no reason in the words is noise.
- **Their words.** Hook type is the sentence they say, on frame 0, in step with their voice.
- **Real things first.** Their screen, their files, the actual article. Anything invented is labelled "Sample".
- **Sound on every event, under the voice.** A silent graphic feels unfinished.
- **Readable on a phone.** Nothing under 30 px that matters. Nothing below y 1470 or within the right 100 px from y 1155 down, where the app's buttons sit. Top 220 px is risky too.
- **The frame test.** Pause on any frame: does it look designed or like a mistake? Look at the frames yourself before showing them.

Restraint is part of it: one look, one transition style, one text style. Flex mode is the exception, and even there, busy is the brief while slop isn't.
